# Deviations from CSwFP

A running log of the points where `CSwL` diverges from the book's structure —
whether forced by presenting in Lean instead of Haskell, or by the
instructor's own decisions. Grows with each chapter; see also the swapped
numbering of chapters 2/3, already noted in
[Conventions](README.md#conventions) in the README.

| Where | CSwFP | CSwL | Reason |
|------|-------|------|--------|
| Ch. 3 | `Rel` (binary relation) only appears in §2.2 of the book | first use of `Rel` is in chapter 3, not chapter 2 | Lean vs Haskell — `IntroL.lean` (chapter 2) imports nothing on purpose; `Rel` is `Mathlib.Data.Rel`, and chapter 2 only gets its first Mathlib import in chapter 3 |
| Ch. 3 | §2.5 "Types in Grammar and Computation" mixes the motivation for types with the categorial reading (`S → NP VP`) in a single section | the two stay at the same `##` level, neither subordinate to the other | instructor's decision — the previous version of the chapter subordinated the categorial reading as a `###`, hiding that it is the section's result, not an addendum |
| Ch. 1–3 | build with no warnings at all | `sorry` is allowed in `CSwL/`, not only in exercises, when left there on purpose | instructor's decision — some chapter examples are deliberately left with `sorry`, to be solved live in class instead of arriving finished |
| Ch. 1–3 | exercises live in a separate `Exercises/` file, one per chapter | exercises moved inside the chapter, at the position of the section they correspond to | instructor's decision — reduces duplication between the chapter and the exercise file, and shows the dependency between an exercise and the section that motivates it |
| Ch. 1–2 | sections with no explicit numbering, or numbered only in the exercise file | `## N.M Title`, aligned with the corresponding `CSwFP` section; sections that exist only in `CSwL`, with no counterpart in the book, stay unnumbered | instructor's decision — makes it easy to check the correspondence between chapter and book; the numbering is the `CSwL` chapter's own (see Conventions), not the book's absolute numbering |
| Ch. 2 | sections numbered using the book's numbering (`## 3.3`–`## 3.14`) | renumbered `## 2.1`–`## 2.15`, `CSwL`'s own — each `##` carries a `Ref. CSwFP/3 §3.N` line pointing at the book section | instructor's decision — the book's numbering textually collided with `Foundation.lean`'s (both used `## 3.N`, referring to different book chapters); aligns chapter 2 with the same pattern chapters 1 and 4 already followed |
| Ch. 2 | §3.9 Type Classes / §3.11 Finnish vowel harmony / §3.14 Phonemes, in the book's order (§3.9 before §3.11 and §3.14) | "Finnish vowel harmony and Swedish plural" (`## 2.14`) and "Application: representing phonemes" (`## 2.15`) moved to the end of the numbered sequence, after "Type classes" (`## 2.11`) | instructor's decision — the two NLP examples are grouped at the end of the chapter, as an application of what comes before, instead of interleaved in the book's order |
| Ch. 2 | book does not cover `Prop` or formal proof in this chapter | new section "Propositions and proofs" (`## 2.2`), with no `Ref.` to CSwFP/3 | instructor's decision — `Foundation.lean` uses `Prop` from the first lines without explaining it; the section covers the minimum needed to read it, with a tactic table and term-construction exercises modeled on `FAA2025/Lectures/Week01-02/Sheet*.lean` and `logical_verification_2026/lean/LoVe/LoVe01_TypesAndTerms_Demo.lean` |
| Ch. 2 | 14 of the chapter's exercises were labeled `E0.n`, out of sequential order in the file | all 14 get `### Exercise* — <title>`, unnumbered | record correction — checked against `CSwFP/txt/chapter-03.txt` that none of the 14 corresponds to a real exercise in the book (which has `Exercise 3.1`–`3.19`); they were instructor placeholders, inherited from when the material lived in `Exercises/` |
| Ch. 2 | Exercises 3.18 (p. 54) and 3.19 (p. 61), real ones from the book | promoted to `### Exercise 3.18`/`### Exercise 3.19`, previously missing from the chapter | record correction — the supporting material (`fValue`/`fMatch`) was already written; only the exercise itself was missing |
| Ch. 2 | "User-defined Data Types" (§3.13) introduces `data`/`inductive` | "Inductive types" (`## 2.5`, the general `inductive`) comes before "Recursion" (`## 2.6`) | Lean vs Haskell — recursion over `Nat`/`List` needs `inductive` to already have been introduced; the book can defer this to §3.13 because Haskell does not require declaring the inductive type first |
| Ch. 2 | `opaque`/`Option` have no section of their own in the book | `opaque` appears in `## 2.1` (together with types and terms); `Option` appears in `## 2.8` (after inductive types and lists) | instructor's decision — follows the presentation pattern of `logical_verification_2026/lean/LoVe/LoVe01_TypesAndTerms_Demo.lean` for `opaque`, and groups `Option` with the `{α : Type}` that `Option α` uses |
| Ch. 4 | §4.4 gives `Cnj [Form]`/`Dsj [Form]` (Haskell) over a binary BNF | `Form` with binary constructors (`conj`, `disj`) + `Form.conjs`/`Form.disjs : List Form → Form` for the n-ary notation | Lean vs Haskell — a list inside the type itself is *nested*: it loses `deriving DecidableEq` and the `induction` tactic, which Exercises 4.11/4.15 (unique reading) require; measured, not assumed |
| Ch. 4 | `Cnj []`/`Dsj []` shown as `"true"`/`"false"` (§4.6, book's comment) | `Form` gets its own constructors `top`/`bot`, not atoms named `"⊤"`/`"⊥"` | instructor's decision — a named atom would depend on chapter 5's value assignment; `top`/`bot` are constants, always `true`/`false` |
| Ch. 4 | board rows numbered 1 to 10 | `Row := Fin 10`, 0 to 9 — the book's row is `Row` minus 1 | Lean vs Haskell — `Fin 10` does not reject the literal `10` (`OfNat` wraps it to `0`); only the validated constructor (`⟨n, by omega⟩`) rejects `n ≥ 10`; the chapter's prose names the offset explicitly |
| Ch. 4 | `Sent`/`NP`/`RCN`/`VP`/`INF` (Haskell) derive `Eq` | the five mutually recursive types do not derive `DecidableEq`, only `Repr` | Lean vs Haskell — `deriving DecidableEq` over a `mutual` block of 5 types generates harmless `termination_by` warnings; verified that `FSynF.hs` itself only derives `Show`, and no later chapter compares these values for equality |
| Ch. 4 | §4.3 has no Haskell code (deferred to chapter 5) | `inductive Query`/`Statement` already in Lean | instructor's decision — a cheap deviation that chapter 5 takes advantage of |
| Ch. 4 | `Sheer` in `FSynF.hs:53` | `cheer` | typo in the distributed source — the book's text (p. 71) prints `Cheer`, consistent with `VP.Cheered` |
| Ch. 4 | `Sent`/`NP`/`RCN`/`VP`/`INF` derive only `Show` (prints the constructor, e.g. `Sent (NP2 The (RCN2 ...))`) | hand-written `ToString` returns the surface English sentence (`"the dwarf that Snow White helped..."`) | instructor's decision — not what the book does; it is the reverse of parsing (chapter 9), included here as a preview, not as a translation of the book's `Show` |
